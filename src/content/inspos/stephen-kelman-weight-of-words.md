### What happens
A near-black A4 poster (about #161616) sits centred on a mid-grey page. White sans type: a row of four small columns at the top (format and material notes in English and German), "A4" at the left, the title "The Weight of Words" and its German version "Das Gewicht der Wörter" below, two short text columns, and two lines of small type at the foot. A small line of vertical text runs along the right edge. Only the poster moves.

0.0-1.0s it holds still and readable.
1.0-2.0s the four columns at the top start to smear: each letter is stretched into a white vertical streak that runs down.
2.0-3.0s the streaks lengthen and the top text is largely gone. The title starts to lose letters ("The Weig", "of Words" partly eaten).
3.0-4.5s the German title and the two body columns break up too, and a bright mass of vertical streaks builds in the middle of the poster, reaching close to the foot.
4.5-6.0s the mass drops. It gets shorter, sinks toward the bottom edge, and the streaks spill out past the poster's bottom edge onto the grey.
6.0-7.0s only a few streaks are left at the bottom, with a little of the foot text still readable among them.
7.0-9.6s an empty dark poster. It does not loop in the footage.

### Timing and feel
About 1s of stillness, about 5s of dissolving, and the last 2.6s empty. The movement is downward only and speeds up as the text breaks apart, like type falling under its own weight. It is slow at first and the text goes in order, top to bottom, header first and foot text last. Easing is hard to read at 2 frames a second. It looks like ease-in.

### What it is made of
Black ground, white type in one grotesque sans, and white vertical streaks that look like each glyph stretched or dragged downward and blurred, brightest where letters overlap. Faint grain on the black. The poster's rectangle stays fixed and the streaks are not clipped by its bottom edge. I can't tell from the frames how the streak effect was made.

### To recreate the look
Inferred from the footage, untested.
1. Set a restrained poster in white type on black, with plenty of space and a clear reading order.
2. Hold it still long enough to be read before anything moves.
3. Stretch each glyph vertically into a streak, starting with the topmost lines, and let the streaks run down and brighten where they overlap.
4. Accelerate the fall, let the mass sink and shorten, let it overflow the bottom edge, then drain to an empty ground.
