### What happens
The poster's words: "A Collaborative Animation Experiment. Jake Fried + 40 Artists. NFT NYC 2026. Scroll down to see all the amazing work."

The full frame is a grid of small square drawings, 6 across and 8 down, each holding one hand-drawn eye with a black pupil. Each square is a different style: heavy black marker lids on pale paper, yellow, red, green, magenta and blue fills, patterns, hearts and horns, a few with tiny words. At 12s the clip shows the same grid.

Every cell changes on the same beat. The picture holds for two video frames, then all 48 cells swap at once. That is 12 changes a second, the pace of animation drawn on twos. Between changes the frame is perfectly still. Looking at the first frames, a cell does not wobble from one version of its own eye to the next. It cuts to a different drawing, with different colours and marks, so each cell reads as a short flipbook of separate eyes. I can't tell from the frames whether one artist drew each cell or whether the drawings are shuffled between cells.

The set repeats every 12 changes, which is exactly 1 second. The 1-second loop runs 12 times to fill the clip. I compared whole frames only, so a slower variation inside one cell would not show up.

### Timing and feel
Jittery, hand-made. 12 drawings per second, every cell in sync, no easing because nothing slides. No camera move and no interaction. Hard loop of one second, repeated.

### What it is made of
Scans or photos of marker and pen on paper, cropped square and tiled with a thin grey seam (about #c8c8c8, approximate). Black ovals for the eye, many with a half-lid. Bright primary and pink fills, with black as the dominant colour. The paper has a visible grain and the video is compressed, so fine hatching is soft. The colour is all in the drawings.

### To recreate the look
Inferred from the footage, untested.
1. Give each contributor the same prompt, here an eye in a square, and a short fixed number of drawings, here 12.
2. Tile them in a strict grid with very thin gutters, and play all cells together.
3. Hold each drawing for two frames at 24fps, so the whole wall changes together on 12 steps a second.
4. Loop the 1-second set. The variety between cells carries it, so leave the layout plain.
