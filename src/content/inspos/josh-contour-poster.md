### What happens
The poster's post says only "WIP", with no method. This is what the frames show. A tall poster sits centred on black. The cursor is visible in the last frame, so it looks like a screen recording of a page, but nothing shows what drives it.

About 17 posters run one after another, each held for roughly 1.5-2s. Each is a very large cropped letter drawn as banded contours, with a short title, small vertical type and a counter bottom left. In order of appearance: "Easy does it" (S, yellow with blue and orange), "Thank you" (U, acid yellow), "Lost & found" (Q, dark purple with yellow and pink), "Hello hello" (lime with pink), "Checking in" (grey with pink and orange), "Round numbers" (blue with orange), "Everyone welcome" (E or H, orange), "Warm regards" (W, dark green with a green-white outline), "Small talk" (K, pale pink), "Mild weather" (R, yellow with blue), "Good neighbors" (S, yellow), "Friendly sans" (G, dark purple), "Soft power" (H, lime), "Nice to meet you" (Y, grey), "Stay a while" (W, blue), then "Easy does it" again in orange (H).

Within one poster the bands drift slowly. Between 0.2s and 2.2s on the first poster the blue band thickens and the S curves shift a little, and the bottom-left number changes (522 to 467).

### What it is made of
A poster frame (about 3:4) on pure black. Fields of saturated colour with a fine ridged line texture along the band edges, like a topographic map. Each palette is only a few hues: yellow, blue, orange, hot pink, lime, dark purple, plus one grey and one green-glow version. Title top left in a bold sans, two or three lines. Two small vertical lines of text, one bold, one tiny with a long sentence. A number and a short bar at bottom left. Any grain or glow beyond the colour bands: not clear.

### To recreate the look
Inferred from the footage, untested.
- Pick one letter per poster and crop it so large that only a curve or a stem shows.
- Fill the letter's surroundings with soft colour bands, hot at the edge and cooler away from it, and add fine ridged lines where bands meet.
- Use 3-4 hues per poster, a different set each time, and change the ground colour too.
- Keep the type small and fixed in the same places on every poster so only the image changes.
- Let the bands creep slowly for about 2 seconds, then cut hard to the next poster.
- Put a number and short bar bottom left that changes with the motion.
